title: Another agent wrote her top lesson

subtitle: A job-screening judge learns from the reasons its owner gives when she rejects a role. Its strongest lesson about her, pay below her floor, was eight times "her" reason, and seven of those eight were a staging script's instructions for filling in a form
concepts: label-provenance, advisory-feedback, verify-from-logs
symptom: A read-only audit of how the operation's agents share knowledge opened the screening judge's live lesson file, the summary it reads on every job it evaluates. The lesson list is built from the reasons the operator writes on CRM deals she rejects. Its pay lesson read "Pay below her floor — 8x", and the quoted examples were not opinions. They were the opening of a note that starts "The form pre-fills 'Your rate: $1/hour'. Change it before Next." That note is written by a separate staging script that prepares contract-platform applications for her. The judge had been carrying it as one of her most frequent reasons for saying no.
root_cause: The learner reads every note on a rejected deal and labels it as her reason, except notes that match a list of known agent templates. The list had grown by hand as agent note types were noticed: the document-kit notes on 29 September and a browser-prompt note on 1 October. The staging script that writes the form instructions had been writing since 23 September, from a different repository, and was never added to the list, so its note was trusted as hers. Its text repeats on every deal it stages, so the mislabel did not add noise: it produced one of the most frequent lessons in the file. A second half kept it there: the derived reasons are cached per deal, and a deal is only re-read when it changes, so even a corrected exclusion would not have touched the seven entries already learned.
fix: Two changes inside the existing learner, no new component. The staging script's note type joins the existing list of agent templates. And a cached reason that now matches that list is derived again from the deal's notes on the next run, so the seven borrowed reasons are recomputed instead of kept. The judge itself, the lesson format and the operator's own notes were not touched.
verified: From the learner's own output on its next scheduled run after deploy. Its ledger line went from "389 rejections, 54 with her reason, refreshed 0" to "389 rejections, 47 with her reason, refreshed 7". The staging note's phrase occurred 7 times in the decisions file and 6 times in the lesson file before the run, and 0 and 0 after it. The pay lesson now reads "Pay below her floor — 1x", and its one example is her own sentence that the salary was too low. The deployed learner is byte-identical to the committed version on the laptop. The rest of the same audit was handled the same way: one change per proven gap, each double-checked by two independent reviewers before deploy, and two candidates held back because the review found that turning them on would have created a new problem.
rule: A learner that reads human feedback from a shared record must know who wrote each line. Every time a new agent starts writing where the learner reads, register that agent's note type in the same change, and re-derive the labels already cached from it. Check by reading the learner's top lessons with their quotes, not by counting lessons: a lesson whose quotes are instructions rather than opinions was borrowed from a machine.
