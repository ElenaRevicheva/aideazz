title: The filter overruled the judge
slug: 2026-10-26-the-filter-overruled-the-judge
date: 2026-10-26
blog: yes
subtitle: A job-search agent's AI rated an employer's video-screen invitation 1.0 positive, and a one-line keyword guard behind it dropped the message as an auto-reply. A recruiter's message, rated 0.95, was dropped for coming from a no-reply address. Both were then remembered as already handled
concepts: downstream-veto, ack-is-not-completion, silent-failure, verify-from-logs
symptom: On 26 October 2026 an employer invited the owner to a recorded video screen with a five-day expiry, and a recruiter at a talent platform wrote to offer a role. Neither produced the Telegram alert or the CRM note that a positive reply is supposed to produce. The agent's log showed it had seen both: at 08:20:38 UTC it logged the invitation as a positive response, then at 08:20:52 "auto-reply (not an interview), NOT alerting"; at 13:30:07 it logged the recruiter's message as positive, then at 13:30:26 "blocked sender, NOT alerting/pushing". Its database held both with their scores: positive 1.0 and positive 0.95.
root_cause: After the AI classifier decides a mail is a hot reply, the agent runs two older guards that were added to cut real noise. The first drops anything containing phrases such as "thank you for applying", which mass confirmations use. The invitation began "Thank you for applying" and went on, "As a next step, this role includes a video screen… will expire 5 days after receiving this email." The guard read the first sentence and not the rest. The second guard drops senders whose address contains "no-reply", which newsletters and platform bots use; the recruiter's platform sends real recruiter messages from exactly such an address. Each guard ran after the classifier and outranked it, whatever its confidence. A second fault made the drop permanent: the message was saved as "handled" before the guards ran, so every later hourly cycle logged "already alerted, skipping" for the invitation (09:22, 10:24, 11:26 and on) although nothing had ever been sent.
fix: Mail that names a concrete next step — a video screen, an assessment, a deadline, an invitation, a request to book a time — is no longer treated as an auto-reply, whatever courtesy phrase it opens with; the plain "we'll be in touch with next steps" of an ordinary confirmation still is. The recruiter platform was exempted from the no-reply rule, and a positive mail that a guard still drops is now reported to the owner in one quiet line instead of only to the log. The next day the same shape showed up elsewhere: a 30-day log count found 22 more mails the classifier had rated hot dropped only for saying "no-reply" — from three companies' applicant-tracking relays and one firm's hiring address, a mix of job-match notices and application follow-ups. So the no-reply rule was narrowed for good: a sender caught only by a generic word now alerts when the classifier is at least 80% sure, while sources blocked by name stay blocked, and an alert that fails to send is now queued and retried instead of being remembered as done.
verified: From the service log and its database. Before: the two lines quoted above, the two stored scores (1.0 and 0.95), and the hourly "already alerted, skipping" lines for the invitation after the first drop. The fix was deployed with a service restart at 14:29:38 UTC on 26 October, with tests for the next-step phrases, the plain confirmation that must still be treated as one, and the exempted sender. The follow-up of 27 October ships with tests that a failed alert is queued and later delivered and that named noise stays blocked; a one-time catch-up message listing the five hot mails the old rule had hidden in the previous 14 days was delivered to the owner's Telegram at 14:00:56 UTC. No new mail of this shape had arrived by the time of writing, so the live path after the second deploy is proven by tests, not yet by traffic.
rule: When a careful judgement is followed by cheap guards, decide the order of authority on purpose: confident evidence should outrank a keyword. And never record an item as handled before the step that handles it has run.
