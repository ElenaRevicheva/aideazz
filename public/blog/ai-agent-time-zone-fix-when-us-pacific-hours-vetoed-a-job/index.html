AI Agent Time Zone Fix: When US Pacific Hours Vetoed a Job

October 13, 2026 · by Elena Revicheva

My job judge AI agent vetoed a candidate for Epoch AI on October 7th, citing "three hours of overlap with Pacific Time." This was a direct misinterpretation of time zones. Panama operates on UTC-5 all year. US Pacific Time (UTC-8) from 9 AM to 5 PM translates to 12 PM to 8 PM in Panama. This is a 7-hour overlap, not 3, and it's perfectly compatible. The agent, despite being explicitly prompted with the correct time zone context, failed to apply it.

The Veto Trigger and Initial Misunderstanding

The VibeJobHunterAIPA_AIMCF agent, responsible for evaluating job applications, rejected a candidate for Epoch AI. The specific reason logged on October 7th was a perceived time zone incompatibility. The agent's internal logic, even with the prompt line, was not correctly mapping US Mountain/Pacific hours to Panama's UTC-5. This wasn't a subtle error; it was a fundamental miscalculation of time differences. The agent was designed to filter for specific working hours, but its interpretation of those hours across time zones was flawed.

Enforcing Time Zone Compatibility in Code

On October 12th, I committed two fixes to VibeJobHunterAIPA_AIMCF. The first commit, 7b4a975, explicitly stated that "US Mountain/Pacific hours are compatible with Panama (UTC-5 all year: Pacific 9-17 = 11-19 Panama)." This was a direct response to the October 7th veto. However, the agent vetoed Epoch AI again on October 12th, even with this prompt line. This confirmed that relying solely on prompt engineering for such a critical, deterministic rule was insufficient.

The second commit, 6a0a94e, addressed this by enforcing the rule directly in the code. The commit message reads: "fix(judge): enforce in code that US Mountain/Pacific hours fit Panama - the live judge vetoed Epoch AI again on 12 Oct WITH the prompt line, so like the LATAM rule the criterion is overruled in code (veto stands for residence, country lock, a range without UTC-5, or non-US hours); tests." This meant that for specific time zone compatibility, the agent's internal decision-making process was overridden by a hard-coded rule. The agent can still veto based on residence, country lock, or non-US hours, but the specific US Mountain/Pacific to Panama time zone conversion is now a non-negotiable compatibility.

Broader Implications for AI Agent Reliability

This incident highlights a recurring challenge in AI agent development: the gap between explicit instructions in a prompt and the agent's actual operational logic. While large language models are powerful, their interpretation of nuanced, deterministic rules, especially those involving numerical calculations like time zones, can be unreliable. My cto-aipa process, for example, has seen 201 restarts, indicating ongoing adjustments and refinements. The algom-stream process has 55193 restarts, showing a different scale of operational volatility.
