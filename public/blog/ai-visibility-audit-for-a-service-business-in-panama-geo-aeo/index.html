AI Crawler Access group (25% weight)

This group tests whether the major AI bots can reach and read the page at all. Eleven checks matter here.

Server returns a readable page (HTTP 200) is the first gate. robots.txt reachable follows. Then come the specific bot allowances: GPTBot for ChatGPT, OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT Search, ClaudeBot for Claude, PerplexityBot for Perplexity, Google-Extended for Gemini, and CCBot for training corpora.

The audit also looks for llms.txt, XML sitemap available, and Page is indexable (no noindex meta/header). If any of these eleven fails, the other three groups become irrelevant for that model.

For a Panama service business this group decides whether your offer appears when someone asks “who helps executives move from Russia to Panama on an ordinary family budget.” No crawl permission equals zero visibility.

Structured Data (GEO) group (25% weight)

GEO here means Generative Engine Optimization, the structured signals that help AI systems map your business to real-world entities and answers.

Eight checks run. Structured data present (JSON-LD / microdata) is the base. Identity schema (Organization / Person / WebSite) tells the model who you are. Answer-rich schema (FAQ / Article / Product / Service) supplies the actual content the model can quote.

Entity links (sameAs to profiles / knowledge graph) connect you to Wikidata or LinkedIn so the model trusts the identity. Open Graph tags (title, description, image), Canonical URL declared, Language declared on , and Meta description (50–170 chars) complete the set.

A service business in Panama needs the language declaration and the sameAs links most. Without them the model cannot confidently tie your Spanish-language offer to the Panama GEO entity. Answer-Readiness (AEO) group (30% weight)

AEO stands for Answer Engine Optimization. This is the largest slice because it directly measures whether your page can become the quoted answer.

Nine checks. Title tag (15–70 chars) must exist and be concise. Exactly one H1 prevents model confusion. Section headings (H2/H3) structure content, and Question-style headings (FAQ signal) tell the model this page answers real queries.

Content depth (300+ words) is the minimum the audit accepts; I do not have deeper thresholds measured. Semantic HTML5 landmarks, Lists or tables (extractable facts), and Freshness signal (dates) round out the group.

For a service business the question-style headings and lists matter most. When Perplexity or Claude builds a “best relocation consultant in Panama” answer it pulls from pages that already look like FAQs.
