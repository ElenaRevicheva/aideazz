Building Production TypeScript with Claude Code: A Non-Technical Founder’s Journey

November 9, 2026 · by Elena Revicheva

I launched into production with a staggering number of failures. Six out of nine supervised processes were online, while the algom-stream had undergone a catastrophic 14,192 restarts, indicating severe instability. I had to face the harsh reality that I was operating without the technical acumen of a computer science degree, yet I relied on my AI assistant, Claude Code, like a permanent pair programmer.

Understanding AI as an Extension, Not a Shortcut

In system development, especially as a non-technical founder, the popular conception often treats AI as a miraculous shortcut. This is misleading. Claude Code does not replace my decision-making but amplifies it. For instance, in my last two days of commits, while I executed 12 changes in cto-aipa, Claude Code allowed me to maintain continuity in complex iterations, writing boilerplates and establishing code patterns I would have struggled with. Still, he cannot autonomously build features; I must guide, review, and iterate.

Where I Trust AI Blindly

There are specific tasks I delegate to Claude Code without supervision, notably repetitive boilerplate tasks and initial drafts of feature specifications. For example, I asked him for a breakdown of user story templates and received a solid draft that saved me hours. Yet, I cautiously avoid trusting him with critical logic implementation or architectural decisions. This is evident in the two restarts and minor bugs that came up during the integration of new features he recommended for privacy policy drafting.

Relying on Proven Processes

Much of AI-assisted development has to do with the processes and frameworks you put in place. Out of the 9 processes I monitor using PM2, eight were reported online. My active processes include essential components like pm2-logrotate, which has zero restarts over four days and maintains a lean memory footprint at 85 MB. This signals reliability in what I can produce through Claude's aid, representing the backbone of my operations.

I strictly follow a methodical approach to development that embraces Claude Code's capabilities while also requiring me to robustly oversee operational workflows. This balance ensures that neither the AI nor I gets overwhelmed. I segregate tasks into two camps — AI-driven tasks, where Claude excels, and critical decision-making tasks where my direct input remains paramount.

Fetching Insights from Git Commits

When delving into code management, the ability to fetch answers from my Git commit history proves invaluable. The last 48 hours logged 12 commits, showcasing my active engagement with Claude Code as we worked through features, especially around drafting Google Play registration requirements. This information isn't just a recitation; I rely heavily on specific instances where productive discussions yield actionable insights.

For instance, one commit from November 9, 5791ccb1, highlights a successfully drafted privacy policy, revealing how Claude can assist in legal documents where I am less confident. This collaboration aids in rapid cycles of iteration yet still requires my scrutiny to ensure robustness against potential pitfalls.

The Value of Data-Driven Feedback

Data informs my trust in Claude Code. For example, I track the performance of my processes routinely. Out of my current processes, 85 MB and 80 MB of memory use in both pm2-logrotate and dragontrade-dashboard signify efficiency at play. If Claude backs my execution with even a fraction of this efficiency within our collaborative space, I feel more confident in allocating more tasks his way.

However, when I noted algom-tream waiting for a restart after a staggering 14,192 failures, that’s a glaring red flag. AI may accelerate processes, but stalwart operations often depend on rigorous oversight and adjustment. I need to know when AI output stumbles, as in real-world applications; failure can be costly.

Why Agile Iteration Is a Non-Negotiable

Amidst the havoc often observed with first-time AI integration, agile practices are vital. The balance of deploying AI to "assist" rather than "replace" ensures a commanding grip over development outcomes. An example is using iterative cycles to handle the cto-aipa project through 12 commits in just 48 hours. Guiding Claude through structured feedback loops helps him refine coding decisions he recommends, like implementation patterns or logical flows without detracting from my directive role.

Without these continuous checks and an understanding of the limitations inherent in Claude as an assistant, I risk a disjointed evolution of my project, relinquishing quality control to what I have termed machine-generated content instead of collaborative intelligence.

Stretching the Capacity of AI

Today, Claude Code's utility spans several APIs and techniques; however, I must control the narrative. With processes supervised by PM2, I know where to capitalize on AI's prowess without blind faith. My committed approach—aligning precise human oversight with intelligent automation—delivers a compounded edge.

For instance, the audit results from my last checks scored a perfect 100 on a visibility audit run against my project site. This outcome solidifies my belief in Claude's ability to complement our hard work—projecting visibility and scalability, devoid of over-reliance on the automaton's insights.

In the end, my journey as a non-technical founder has been fraught with challenges, but learning to coexist with AI has enshrined my adherence to rigorous workflows while pulling through production challenges through technology.
