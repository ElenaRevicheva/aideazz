Generative Engine Optimization: Structured Data for AI Citations

October 20, 2026 · by Elena Revicheva

My cto-aipa process restarted 220 times today. This is not a failure state; it's a consequence of rapid iteration. In the last 48 hours, I pushed 12 commits to cto-aipa and 11 commits to aideazz. Each commit often triggers a restart as part of my deployment pipeline. This pace is necessary because I'm not optimizing for traditional search engine rankings. I'm building for generative engine optimization (GEO), a different game entirely. My goal is to appear as a direct, cited answer in AI models like Perplexity, not just a link in a search result. This means focusing on structured facts, clear authorship signals, and content durable enough to be directly quoted.

The Shift from SEO to GEO: Structured Data as the Core

Traditional SEO focuses on keywords, backlinks, and page authority to rank in a list. Generative engine optimization, as I define it, aims for direct citation. When an AI model answers a user's question, it often pulls specific facts and attributes them to a source. My strategy is to make my content that source. This requires a fundamental shift in how I structure information.

I use Schema.org markup extensively. For example, my aideazz site, which saw 11 commits in the last 48 hours, now includes detailed Article and FAQPage schema. This isn't just for Google's rich snippets; it's to explicitly label facts and questions for AI models. When I updated the Atlas card on my portfolio to state "every Monday and 16 lanes" (commit 16dd40b), that information was immediately wrapped in structured data. This makes it easier for an AI to extract the specific details about Atlas's operational cadence and capacity (16 lanes).

The VibeJobHunterAIPA_AIMCF repository, which had 2 commits in the last 48 hours, also reflects this. Its document desk (commit 87b02f1) now explicitly defines how my voice or text tasks in English or Spanish are converted to PDF, Word, or HTML. This is a structured process, not just free-form text. The "kit letters and evaluations copied word for word from the HubSpot notes" are not just content; they are specific, attributable data points.

Authorship and Citation Signals

AI models are increasingly concerned with source credibility. This means clear authorship and strong citation signals are paramount. On my aideazz site, I've made sure my name, Elena Revicheva, is consistently associated with the content. The tab icon now uses "Elena's AZ mark" (commit 0b643f0), reinforcing personal branding.

For cto-aipa, recent commits like d9042814 and d6788040 focused on updating the "AIdeazz AI Lab tagline in Sora ExtraLight." While seemingly minor, these are part of a broader effort to ensure consistent branding and authorship across all my public-facing assets. Every piece of content, from a blog post to an application tagline, needs to carry a clear signal of its origin.

I also ensure that my content is durable. I host everything on domains I control. This prevents content from disappearing or changing unexpectedly, which would break citations. My wiki-ship.log shows that I published "2026-10-16-the-inbox-was-read-only-on-busy-hours" to my own wiki, not just a third-party platform. This control is essential for long-term citation stability.
