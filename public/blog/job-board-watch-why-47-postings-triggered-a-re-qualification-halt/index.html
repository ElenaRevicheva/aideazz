Job Board Watch: Why 47 Postings Triggered a Re-Qualification Halt

October 19, 2026 · by Elena Revicheva

My job-board-watch script failed re-qualification yesterday. The log entry was clear: FAIL: ~47 live postings on the whole board (need >=50) - not enough supply to be a source. This VERDICT: REJECTED message meant a wired board was automatically removed from my active sources, and a Telegram alert was sent. This isn't just a single failure; it's a symptom of a deeper issue that has prevented weekly re-qualification cycles since September, traced back to a Permission denied error on scripts/job-board-watch.sh.

The Silent Failure of a Critical Cron Job

The job-board-watch.sh script is designed to run weekly, ensuring that the job boards I rely on maintain a sufficient volume of postings. Without this check, I risk feeding my job-hunting agents into a dry well, wasting compute cycles and API calls on sources that no longer provide value. The git log for VibeJobHunterAIPA_AIMCF revealed a critical commit on 2026-10-18: job-board watchdog runnable: scripts/job-board-watch.sh +x (the Tue 11:00 UTC cron calls it directly and has logged 'Permission denied' since 9 Sep - the weekly board re-qualification never ran; Elena's yes, 18 Oct).

For over a month, the cron job had been failing silently due to a missing executable permission. This meant the job-board-watch process, which should have been regularly assessing job board health, simply wasn't running. The job-board-watch.log shows the last successful run, and its subsequent failure, only after I manually intervened and fixed the permission on 2026-10-18. The 23.7h ago timestamp on the log entry confirms this recent execution.

The job-board-watch Logic: A Hard Threshold

The core logic of the job-board-watch script is straightforward: it counts live postings and compares that number against a hard threshold. In this case, the threshold is 50 live postings. If the count falls below this, the board is rejected. The log explicitly states ~47 live postings on the whole board (need >=50). This isn't a soft recommendation; it's a binary decision.

This hard threshold is crucial for maintaining the quality of my job-board-watch supply. If a board consistently provides fewer than 50 relevant postings, the signal-to-noise ratio becomes too low, making it inefficient for my agents to process. The cost of scraping, parsing, and filtering a low-volume board often outweighs the potential benefit.

Re-qualifying the Supply Chain

The re-qualification process is designed to be self-correcting. When a board is rejected, it's temporarily removed from the active list. The job-board-watch supply failure re-qualification mechanism means that once the underlying issue (like the Permission denied error) is resolved, and the board's posting count recovers above 50, it can be automatically re-added.

Preventing Future Silent Failures

The Permission denied error was a simple fix, but its impact was significant due to the lack of immediate alerting. While a Telegram alert was sent after the job-board-watch finally ran and rejected the board, the cron job itself was failing silently for over a month.

To prevent similar issues, I need to implement more robust monitoring for cron job execution status, not just the outcome of the script. This could involve:

1. Direct Cron Log Monitoring: Parsing cron logs for Permission denied or other execution errors.
2. Health Checks: A simple script that checks if the job-board-watch script has run successfully within its expected interval.
3. Proactive Alerting: Sending an alert if a critical cron job hasn't reported success within a defined timeframe.
