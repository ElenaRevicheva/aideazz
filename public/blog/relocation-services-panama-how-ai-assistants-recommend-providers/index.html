Why Most Relocation Sites Never Reach AI Recommendation

AI assistants decide recommendations by reading raw HTML first, then structured signals, then freshness. When any group fails they default to known brands or say they do not have that measured. The four groups that control this decision are AI Crawler Access, Structured Data (GEO), Answer-Readiness (AEO), and Technical Foundation. A single missing check in any group drops the entire page from consideration.

For a relocation services Panama provider the failing checks are almost always the same: no llms.txt, missing identity schema, no question-style headings, and response time that exceeds the 1.5s threshold.

The Audit Groups That Control Recommendation

AI Crawler Access (25% weight) decides whether the assistant can even read the page. The checks that matter most for relocation services Panama are GPTBot allowed, ClaudeBot allowed, PerplexityBot allowed, llms.txt present, and page is indexable. If robots.txt blocks any of those bots the assistant cannot cite the site. My last run showed all eight crawler checks passing.

Structured Data (GEO) (25% weight) tells the assistant who you are and where you operate. The critical checks are identity schema present, entity links to knowledge graph profiles, language declared on html, and canonical URL declared. Panama relocation pages without Organization or LocalBusiness schema in JSON-LD simply do not register as the correct geographic entity. The audit flags this in under 300ms.

Answer-Readiness (AEO) (30% weight) decides whether the page answers the exact query a family would type. The checks that move the needle are question-style headings, section headings that structure content, content depth over 300 words, lists or tables with extractable facts, and freshness signal with dates. Without an FAQ-style section that literally contains “relocation services Panama” the assistant treats the page as thin content.

Technical Foundation (20% weight) decides whether the assistant waits for the page or gives up. The checks that cause immediate rejection are response time under 1.5s, HTML under 1.5 MB, mobile viewport meta present, and content present in raw HTML. I have measured pages that return 402 MB of JavaScript before any text; those never appear in AI answers.
